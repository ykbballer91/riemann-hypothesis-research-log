# 09. Prime-logarithm phases: the arithmetic comma track

**STATUS: RIEMANN HYPOTHESIS OPEN**

Recorded 2026-09-30. Date of the recorded report; individual start and finish times are not reconstructed.

## Starting idea

Prime logarithms generate a torus flow. Could nonresonance, long-time phase statistics or pretentious distance control the distinguished phase at which the Möbius sum is read?

## What was tested

Exact finite Fourier identities, Mellin/Perron reconstruction, time-averaged sinc factors, high moments and near returns were checked. The cost of removing smoothing was kept explicit.

## What survived

- The phase-to-arithmetic reconstruction is exact within its stated cutoff and convergence domains.

- Synthetic comparisons show that identical Haar phase statistics can coexist with very different values at the distinguished arithmetic phase. Quantitative Halász input provides logarithmic savings at its stated scale.

## What failed or remains unproved

Equidistribution and root-mean-square time control do not give the required pointwise square-root estimate. Hardy-space boundary evaluation can be unbounded. A normalization by the number of sign configurations is not cancellation of the actual signed sum.

## Why the work moved on

The next track retained the actual prime-product constraint as a Boolean weighted halfspace rather than replacing it by a phase distribution.

## Current scope

Exact bridges and their losses retained; phase statistics alone did not provide the missing estimate.
