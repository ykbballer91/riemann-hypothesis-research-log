## How the question changed

Earlier work investigated an analytic route through finite Weil forms and approximating functions, seeking to restrict every nontrivial zero to the critical line. The limited results of the [joint transfer](2026-10-05-joint-transfer.md), [CORE-S](2026-10-07-core-s.md), and [Cycles 6–17](2026-10-08-cycles-6-17.md) are retained.

Along that route, finite checks and conditional implications still left a bridge: all necessary conditions had to hold on the same sequence. Closing a universal statement required something beyond the individual results. This gap is the starting point for understanding the present state.

The researcher chose B to investigate not only fixed RH but also the axioms from which it can be proved. B is a continuing investigation of ZFC independence; it does not establish that independence is promising. The finite-Weil route is **HOLD**, with its CAP/PAR obligations preserved. No impossibility of that route has been proved.

## Fixed target and two obligations

$T=\mathrm{ZFC}$; $\rho$ is the arithmetic RH sentence fixed in L2-D 1.0.0. It expresses absence of finite arithmetic violations using a fixed finite rational comparison. Usual mathematical reasoning establishes its RH equivalence inside the same ZFC. L6 and L7 do not replace this canonical sentence.

Independence means that neither a statement nor its negation can be proved from the specified axioms. This program records that goal as two consistency obligations. $\operatorname{Con}$ means that the indicated theory has no finite proof of contradiction.

$$I:\operatorname{Con}(T+\neg\rho)\;\text{— OPEN},\qquad II:\operatorname{Con}(T+\rho)\;\text{— OPEN}.$$

The **2 → 2** count classifies research obligations, not a minimum problem count in mathematics or an independence probability. General incompleteness alone does not establish RH's independence. Finite-Weil CAP/PAR also remain OPEN, 2 → 2, in a separate account; they are neither renamed I/II nor combined into four obligations.

## L7: prime-support normalization and precision bounds

This technical summary omits the complete proof. $S(n)$ is the sum of positive divisors of a positive integer $n$, and $H_n$ is its harmonic number. $q(n,k)$ is the finite rational upper bound for $H_n+\exp(H_n)\log H_n$ fixed in L2-D 1.0.0. It uses the original finite exponential and logarithmic sums and rational tail terms; $k$ is the precision input. It has not been replaced with another bound or a new reduced-fraction computation.

For valid inputs $n,k\ge1$, violation is the strict rational comparison

$$V(n,k)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S(n)>q(n,k).$$

The original guards make it false at $n=0$ or $k=0$; $n=1$ gives equality and no violation. The transfer is conditional on a violation, without assuming an accepted violation exists.

### One replacement step

Assume primes $p<r$, $a,e,k\ge1$ and $\gcd(a,pr)=1$, and set

$$R=ar^e,\qquad z=ap^e,\qquad K=z(5R+7).$$

Usual ZFC reasoning establishes, for the same fixed $q$,

$$\frac{q(z,K)}{z}<\frac{q(R,k)}{R},\qquad V(R,k)\Longrightarrow V(z,K).$$

This accounts for a positive margin in the comparison upper bound and the finite-precision error, beyond the increase in divisor-sum ratio when moving to a smaller prime. $K$ is independent of the original $k$; it need not increase $k$.

### Removing prime-support gaps

$C$ is the family of positive integers whose use of a prime implies use of every smaller prime. It includes $1$. It does not require nonincreasing exponents and is not the family of superabundant numbers.

Iterating replacements of missing small primes moves a violation into $C$. Under the premise $V(n,k)$, the output $(n_*,k_*)$ satisfies

$$C(n_*),\qquad 1\le n_*\le n,\qquad V(n_*,k_*),\qquad k_*\le\max(k,12n^2).$$

Finite recursion on the input defines a total normalization; no polynomial-time algorithm is claimed. Global violation preservation is an ordinary argument in ZFC. Its global PA-provability and the corresponding PA equivalence are not certified here.

## Proof transfer with costs in the same system

Natural numbers include $0$ and $m\ge0$. Both the integer and precision inputs are bounded in the finite sections

$$G(m)=\forall n,k<2^m\,\neg V(n,k),\qquad G_C(m)=\forall n,k<2^m\,[C(n)\to\neg V(n,k)].$$

The precision bound gives, in usual ZFC,

$$G_C(2m+4)\Longrightarrow G(m).$$

This is not an equivalence at the same $m$ in the reverse direction.

$P$ is the Hilbert proof-sequence system of the conservative definitional extension of full-strength ZFC fixed in L5. It permits ordinary auxiliary lemmas, cuts and all ZFC axiom schemata. Length $|\pi|$ counts every character in formulas, numerals, inference annotations and newlines. References use binary backward distances; numerals are arithmetic terms based on binary digits. It does not count lines or checking time.

Appending the fixed internal implication and binary boundary evaluation transfers any correct $P$ proof $\pi$ of $G_C(2m+4)$ to a proof $\tau_m(\pi)$ of $G(m)$. With $\ell(m)=\max(1,\operatorname{bitlen}(m))$,

$$|\tau_m(\pi)|\le|\pi|+A\ell(m)^2+B.$$

$A,B$ are fixed independently of $m,\pi$; their numerical values have not been measured. Large values are not counted as free one-character constants, and evaluation of the boundary $2m+4$ is included.

The level is **usual mathematical reasoning inside ZFC and a syntactic length upper bound**. This does not mean a complete Hilbert string or implemented transformer was generated and machine-certified. The ability to transfer proofs differs from establishing that no proof exists. No lower bound for all $P$ proofs, new reduction to finite consistency, or new relative-consistency input has been obtained.

## Prior work and limits of the checks

Extremization of the divisor-sum ratio through smaller-prime replacement has prior literature. Section 5 of [Choie–Lichiardopol–Moree–Solé (2007)](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.591/) treats Hardy–Ramanujan integers, which also impose exponent ordering. Sections 2–3 of [MacArevey, arXiv:2602.15905v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15905v2) discuss monotonicity of the normalized Lagarias upper value and superabundance of a least counterexample. The families, comparisons and finite-precision conditions cannot simply be identified. No academic novelty is claimed in this publication.

Original small-range rational interval checks and separate internal AI review are distinguished from the usual-mathematics global proof. Preliminary comparisons accepted no input violation and are not presented as execution successes for violation preservation. Internal AI review is neither external peer review nor a machine proof.
