# 16. Rates, history and finite-cutoff splitting

**STATUS: RIEMANN HYPOTHESIS OPEN**

Recorded 2026-09-30. Date of the recorded report; individual start and finish times are not reconstructed.

## Starting idea

Could the way support and Fourier cutoffs increase select the arithmetic kernel from the global radical?

## What was tested

The track separated support restriction R_a from periodic Fourier projection P_N, examined prime-power threshold events, and compared diagnostic paths N ≍ a² and N ≍ a³.

## What survived

- For the two-dimensional family span{k,k″}, support-only minimization selects k after normalization. An explicit boundary cancellation improves its rate.

- Full projected energies on every fixed derivative family have unconditional upper bounds tending to zero along the two diagnostic paths. A simple ground projector at the same finite endpoint has no history dependence; prime-power events introduce derivative corners, not jumps of the matrix.

## What failed or remains unproved

Energy upper bounds are not leading asymptotics or a ground-selection theorem. Beyond two derivatives, the leading nullspace has larger dimension. At this stage neither path-dependent nor path-independent limiting selection had been proved.

## Why the work moved on

The next track resolved a hierarchy for each fixed finite derivative family, using higher-order Schur terms and a stronger Fourier-resolution scale.

## Current scope

Rate and finite-endpoint facts retained. Later refinements apply to restricted families and do not retrospectively prove a full-ground statement here.
